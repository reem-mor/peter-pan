# INC-2434 — notification queue backlog after consumer stall (2026-07-02)

**Severity:** P2 · **Duration:** 52 min · **Class:** latency

**What happened:** the notification queue depth grew from a few hundred to
1.4 million messages over 40 minutes. A poison message — a malformed payload
that threw on deserialization — sat at the head of a partition and the consumer
retried it forever instead of dead-lettering it, so that partition made zero
progress while producers kept enqueuing. Users saw notification delivery delayed
by up to 45 minutes; no messages were lost.

**Detection:** queue-depth and consumer-lag alerts (T+8m); oldest-message-age
crossed the SLO. **Resolution:** routed the poison message to the dead-letter
queue and raised consumer concurrency to drain the backlog (T+49m); lag returned
to baseline within ten minutes of clearing the stuck partition.

**Lessons:**
- Cap per-message retries and dead-letter on exhaustion — infinite retry on a
  poison message is a self-inflicted backlog.
- Alert on oldest-message-age, not just depth; depth alone hid the stalled
  partition behind healthy-looking aggregate throughput.
- See the queue-backlog runbook; drain-with-more-consumers only works once the
  head-of-line blocker is cleared.
